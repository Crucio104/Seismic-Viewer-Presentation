-interpret these vast volumes of hypocentral data in near-real time goes beyond

-protection.

-two-dimensional maps, cross-sections, and time-series plots. While individually
-informative, these representations fail to capture the intricate four-dimensional
-nature of seismogenic processes. A cluster of earthquakes migrating along a plane at
-depth, for instance, may appear as a diffuse cloud in plan view yet reveal a coherent
-planar structure when examined in three dimensions and animated over time. This
-gap between the richness of the data and the poverty of conventional visualisation
-tools motivated the development ofSeismic Viewer.
-The project emerged from a direct need identified during an internship at the
-Istituto Nazionale di Geofisica e Vulcanologia (INGV). Researchers studying the
-Campi Flegrei caldera, one of the most densely monitored and potentially hazardous
-volcanic systems in the world, required a tool that could simultaneously display
-spatial distributions, temporal evolution, and structural relationships within seismic
-sequences, all within a fluid, interactive environment.

+8.3 Future Research and Architectural Extensions
+While Seismic Viewer achieves interactive responsiveness for current regional cata-
+logues, several areas present opportunities for future development.
+As dataset sizes continue to grow beyond the regional scale, the current Plotly.js
+[13] rendering pipeline may reach its practical limits. Migrating the frontend to
+8.4 Concluding Remarks 43
+specialised geospatial rendering engines, such as Deck.gl or a custom WebGL pipeline
+built on Three.js, could enable progressive level-of-detail rendering techniques. This
+migration would not only increase the maximum number of renderable points but
+also open the possibility of deploying the application as a fully browser-based web
+service, eliminating the need for local installation.
+A second important direction concerns real-time monitoring integration. The
+current architecture analyses static or periodically updated catalogues, but integrat-
+ing continuous data streaming protocols such as SeedLink or FDSN web services
+could transform the system into a live surveillance dashboard suitable for monitoring
+rooms. This would require extending the WebSocket communication layer to handle
+continuous data ingestion alongside the existing analytical pipeline.
+Finally, future iterations could expand the analytical capabilities by integrating
+focal mechanism visualisations to represent fault kinematics directly in the 3D scene,
+as well as Coulomb static stress transfer maps to analyse how individual earthquakes
+redistribute tectonic stress across neighbouring faults.

-A.2 Algorithmic Pipeline
-The objective of the spatial analysis module is to extract dominant structural
-orientations from unorganized point clouds representing earthquake hypocenters.
-This is achieved through a multi-stage pipeline that first isolates dense clusters
-of seismicity and then determines the geometric properties of the underlying fault
-structures.
-A.2.1 Spatial Clustering
-The system employs DBSCAN to group seismic events that occur in close spatial
-proximity, effectively separating them from isolated, background seismicity. The
-clustering logic evaluates distances in three dimensions using a Euclidean metric,
-allowing it to identify arbitrarily shaped clusters without requiring the user to specify
-the number of groups beforehand. The core implementation relies on the highly
-optimizedscikit-learnlibrary to process tens of thousands of points efficiently.
-A.2.2 Fault Plane Extraction
-For each identified spatial cluster, the system estimates the best-fitting geometric
-plane. This process relies on the RANSAC algorithm to iteratively sample subsets of
-the cluster and discard outliers that do not conform to a planar structure. Once the
-set of robust inliers is identified, SVD is applied to the centered coordinates. The
-normal vector of the fault plane corresponds to the right singular vector associated
-with the smallest singular value, providing a mathematically rigorous definition of
-the structure’s orientation in 3D space.
